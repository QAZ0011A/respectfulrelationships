The Relationship Spectrum

Relationships aren't just good or bad

Every friendship, crush or partner sits somewhere on a spectrum. Most relationships have ups and downs, but knowing where the lines are helps you notice when things start to shift.

HealthyUnhealthyAbusive
Healthy

Equal & respectful

Both people feel safe, heard and free to be themselves.

  • Open, honest communication
  • Trust without checking up on each other
  • Time apart is normal and encouraged
  • Disagreements are talked through calmly
  • Both people can say no without fear
Unhealthy

Out of balance

One or both people start to feel stressed, unsure or unheard.

  • Frequent jealousy or put-downs passed off as "jokes"
  • Pressure to reply instantly
  • Guilt-tripping or silent treatment
  • Hiding things to avoid a fight
  • Arguments that never get resolved
Abusive

Power & control

One person uses fear, threats or force to control the other. This is never okay.

  • Monitoring phones, location or socials
  • Threats, intimidation or physical harm
  • Pressuring or forcing sexual activity
  • Isolating someone from friends and family
  • Threatening to share private images

Unhealthy behaviour often starts small and escalates over time. If something feels off, trust your gut and talk to someone you trust.

Consent & Digital Respect

Consent is ongoing, online and offline

Consent means everyone involved genuinely agrees, whether it's a hug, a photo being posted, or anything physical. A simple way to remember what real consent looks like is FRIES.

F

Freely given

Chosen without pressure, guilt, threats or being drunk or high.

R

Reversible

Anyone can change their mind at any time, even halfway through.

I

Informed

Everyone knows exactly what they're agreeing to.

E

Enthusiastic

A real, clear "yes". Silence, "maybe" or "I guess" are not consent.

S

Specific

Yes to one thing doesn't mean yes to everything.

Healthy digital boundaries

  • You never owe anyone your passwords, passcode or live location.
  • Not replying straight away is normal. You have a life offline.
  • Ask before posting or sharing photos of someone else.
  • Talk about what you're both comfortable sharing about each other online.
  • Blocking, muting or logging off to protect your peace is always okay.

Know the law

In Australia, creating, sending or sharing sexual images of anyone under 18 can be a crime, even if the image is of yourself or was sent willingly.

Sharing or threatening to share someone's intimate images without their consent is also against the law.

If an image of you has been shared, it's not your fault. The eSafety Commissioner can help get it removed.

    How to be an Upstander & get help

    If a friend seems to be walking on eggshells, is suddenly cut off from everyone, or is always anxiously checking their phone, you can make a difference. You don't need to fix it, you just need to show up.

    Notice

    Dropping sport or hobbies, skipping hangouts, seeming anxious or "not themselves".

    Check in privately

    Pick a calm, private moment. Lead with care, not judgement or attacking the other person.

    Listen & believe

    Let them talk. Don't pressure decisions. Remind them it's not their fault.

    Stay connected

    Keep inviting them. Isolation is a control tactic, so your friendship protects them.

    Tell a trusted adult

    Worried about safety? Telling someone isn't snitching, it's looking after a mate.

    Not sure what to say?Try one of these conversation starters.

    "Hey, I've noticed you seem stressed lately. Is everything okay?"
    "You don't have to explain anything, but I'm here if you want to talk."
    "That doesn't sound fair. You deserve to be treated with respect."
    "Want me to come with you to see the wellbeing coordinator?"
